10 Reasons Your Business Isn't Showing Up on Google Maps

If customers can't find you on Maps, it's almost never random. Here's exactly what to check, in order.

1

Your Google Business Profile isn't verified

This is the first thing to rule out, and the one owners skip most often. A Google Business Profile has to complete verification before it's eligible to show up on Search or Maps at all. It's common to set up a profile, add a phone number or menu, and then never finish the verification step — which means the listing simply isn't live yet, no matter how complete it looks on your end.

2

Your profile has been suspended

A suspended profile can vanish from Maps entirely, with no warning banner for customers — it just stops appearing. Suspensions usually trace back to a guideline violation or Google being unable to confirm your business is eligible for a listing at that address. This happens to legitimate businesses too, not just spammy ones, and the fix is going through Google's reinstatement process rather than creating a new profile.

3

Your business information is inconsistent

Check your Name, Address, and Phone number (your "NAP") everywhere they're listed — your website, Google, JustDial, Instagram, anywhere. If your website says "ABC Dental Clinic," your Google listing says "ABC Dental & Implant Centre," and a directory says something else again, that mismatch chips away at Google's confidence in your data. It won't necessarily get you penalized, but consistent information is one of the easiest trust signals to control.

4

You picked the wrong primary category

This is one of the single biggest levers on this list. A dental clinic listed as "Medical Clinic" or "Health Consultant" instead of "Dentist" is competing for the wrong searches entirely. Your primary category is effectively the filter Google uses to decide which searches you're even eligible to show up for — get it wrong, and no amount of reviews or photos will fully compensate.

5

Your business is too new

A brand-new profile rarely ranks well on day one, and that's expected, not a bug. New listings take time to be processed, and visibility builds up alongside the relevance, distance, and prominence signals mentioned earlier — none of which exist yet for a profile with zero history. Google doesn't publish a fixed timeline, so treat the first few weeks as a build-up phase rather than a failure.

6

You're searching from too far away

Maps results are heavily distance-weighted. A dentist might rank #2 for someone two kilometres away, drop to #10 at eight kilometres, and not appear at all from another city. If you're testing your own ranking by searching from your office desk, you're only seeing one data point. Test from a few different locations — or ask friends in different parts of town to check — before assuming something is broken.

7

Your profile isn't fully filled out

An incomplete profile struggles to compete with one that answers every question a customer might have before they even click through.

  • ✓ Services listed with clear descriptions
  • ✓ A written business description, not left blank
  • ✓ Accurate working hours, including holidays
  • ✓ Recent photos of the business, staff, and work
  • ✓ Products, if applicable to your business type
  • ✓ Questions & Answers, kept current
  • ✓ Relevant attributes (wheelchair access, women-led, etc.)

Not every field is a direct ranking lever, but together they give Google — and your customer — more reason to trust the listing.

8

You have very few reviews, or old ones

Reviews don't guarantee a ranking boost, but they're a real part of your prominence signal, and they're the first thing a customer scans before calling. Three things matter here: how many reviews you have, how recent they are, and whether you respond to them professionally. A profile with two five-star reviews from 2023 reads very differently from one with steady, recent activity.

9

Your website has weak local SEO

It's easy to assume Maps ranking is entirely about the Google Business Profile itself, but your website plays a supporting role.

A

Location pages

A dedicated page for each area or branch you serve, with real local content — not a copy-pasted template.

B

Speed & mobile-friendliness

Most local searches happen on a phone; a slow site loses the click before it loads.

C

Local keywords & schema markup

Mentioning your service area naturally in copy, backed by structured data, helps Google connect your site to local intent.

D

Consistent contact details

The same NAP on your site as on your Google profile — no exceptions.

10

Your competitors simply have stronger signals

Sometimes nothing on your profile is technically broken — a competitor just outranks you on prominence. That usually means more reviews, a stronger website, more local backlinks, better content, a longer track record, or a simply more complete profile. Google's goal is to surface the most relevant and prominent result for that search, so catching up often means improving several of these areas together rather than fixing one setting.

"If your business isn't showing up on Google Maps, don't assume Google is broken."

In almost every case we've audited, the issue traces back to one of these ten checkpoints — verification, category, consistency, completeness, reviews, or competition. The fix is rarely a mystery once you know where to look.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps at all?

The most common causes are an unverified or suspended Google Business Profile, a wrong primary category, inconsistent business information across the web, or simply being outranked by competitors with stronger local signals. Verification and suspension status are the first things to rule out.

How long does it take for a new Google Business Profile to start ranking?

Google doesn't guarantee a timeline. New profiles are typically processed within a few days, but building real visibility depends on relevance, distance, and prominence signals that build up over weeks, not hours.

Does the number of reviews affect Google Maps ranking?

Reviews are one of several local signals that influence prominence, but they don't guarantee ranking on their own. Review count, recency, and how professionally you respond all contribute to trust, alongside relevance and distance.
